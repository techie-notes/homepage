LSTC and Four Partners to Accelerate Medical AI with 2nm Semiconductors, Jointly Developing Three Systems under NEDO Support

A consortium led by the Large Scale Technology Consortium (LSTC) and four partner organizations has secured NEDO (New Energy and Industrial Technology Development Organization) backing to develop medical AI acceleration solutions leveraging cutting-edge 2nm semiconductor processes. The project encompasses the joint development of three systems: AI-assisted medical image diagnosis, clinical decision support, and automated medical document generation. The 2nm process node is expected to deliver performance gains of several to tens of times compared to current generation implementations, driven by enhanced transistor density and reduced power consumption.

The primary bottleneck for medical AI adoption in clinical environments is not algorithm accuracy alone, but rather the feasibility of real-time inference. Medical imaging applications require convolutional neural network inference completion within seconds—a constraint that existing processors struggle to meet within practical cost parameters. The 2nm generation doubles transistor density and substantially improves cache efficiency, enabling AI system deployments at price points accessible to typical healthcare institutions. This bridges the gap between theoretical performance and clinical practicality.

Concurrently, generative AI adoption in healthcare operations is accelerating independently. Gunma University Hospital has reported approximately 40 percent reduction in administrative documentation workload following generative AI implementation, while pharmaceutical companies are similarly streamlining medical document preparation and regulatory review processes. These demonstrated successes create momentum for broader institutional adoption, potentially freeing clinical staff for direct patient care activities.

The project roadmap anticipates implementation phases beginning in 2027 through 2028, with multi-institution validation studies planned accordingly. However, the rapid digitalization of healthcare systems has simultaneously elevated cybersecurity risks, necessitating integrated security infrastructure development alongside AI deployment. This dual-track challenge—advancing clinical AI capabilities while fortifying institutional defenses against cyber threats—has emerged as a critical priority for the entire medical technology sector.
