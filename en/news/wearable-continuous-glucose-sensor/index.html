Needle-Free Continuous Glucose Sensor Enters Final-Stage Trials

September 29, 2026Lumen Biosensing
SOURCELumen Biosensing
STATUSClinical Trial
EVIDENCEPress Release
UPDATED09/29/2026
This article is an AI-generated summary of primary sources.

Lumen Biosensing has moved its needle-free continuous glucose monitor into final-stage accuracy trials, a milestone the company hopes will support a regulatory submission within the next year.

Unlike current CGM systems, which require a subcutaneous filament replaced every one to two weeks, Lumen’s device uses a wrist-worn optical sensor that measures glucose through interstitial fluid spectroscopy, with no skin penetration required.

Early-stage data presented earlier this year showed a mean absolute relative difference within range of existing filament-based CGMs, though the company has acknowledged accuracy during rapid glucose swings — such as immediately after a meal — remains an area of active refinement.

If successful, the trials would position Lumen’s sensor as a direct alternative for the large population of CGM users who cite skin irritation or sensor cost as barriers to continuous use.
