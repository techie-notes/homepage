Philips has released findings from its 2026 Future Health Index survey, demonstrating rapid AI integration throughout clinical settings. The data shows that 50% of healthcare professionals are leveraging AI-assisted diagnostic support, with participating physicians seeing an average of eight patients per week aided by AI tools. Notably, 64% of respondents report personal use of AI applications, indicating that artificial intelligence has become a standard element of medical practice rather than a novel technology.

AI adoption extends well beyond diagnostic support. Samsung-affiliated hospitals in South Korea have developed and deployed AI robots as surgical assistants, automating key steps in operative procedures. Concurrently, Lunit, a Korean medical AI firm, has partnered with Summitherapeuitcs, a U.S. biotech company, to integrate AI into early-stage drug discovery for oncology. The partnership focuses on predicting treatment response and identifying relevant biomarkers—exemplifying how AI is becoming embedded within drug development workflows from inception rather than as a downstream analytical tool.

Remote care infrastructure is expanding across borders and regions. HealthOnCloud, a Korean digital health enterprise, has launched MEDTIS, a cross-border telemedicine platform designed for critically ill patients, and begun global deployment. In Japan, regional healthcare equity initiatives are similarly progressing: Urawa Red Cross Hospital and Sapporo Medical University Hospital have begun remote consultation services, enabling patients in rural Hokkaido to access specialized care previously available only in major urban centers.

These convergent trends underscore a fundamental restructuring of global healthcare delivery. Simultaneous advances in clinical AI deployment, AI-enabled drug discovery, and geographically distributed care systems are creating new pathways for both clinicians and patients. The integration of these technologies suggests that the coming years will see medicine evolve into an increasingly technology-augmented and geographically accessible discipline.
