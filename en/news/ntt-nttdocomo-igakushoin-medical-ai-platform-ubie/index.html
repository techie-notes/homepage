Igaku Shoin, NTT, NTT Docomo Business, and Ubie have announced a collaborative demonstration project for a medical AI information platform designed for reliable deployment in clinical settings. The pilot program will begin in November at Kanto Hospital, enabling the consortium to evaluate the platform’s effectiveness and operational challenges through real-world clinical use. In medical AI development, both technical accuracy and clinical adaptability are essential, making this demonstration a critical milestone toward commercialization.

Meanwhile, AI applications in clinical environments continue to diversify. GE Healthcare Japan is launching a new AI feature called “Lung Guidance” for its handheld ultrasound diagnostic device Vscan starting November 5, enhancing point-of-care diagnostics and enabling faster clinical decision-making at the bedside. Separately, KDDI and Osaka Metro have opened reservation-free online consultation booths in railway stations to provide medical consultations and long-term care advice, improving healthcare accessibility in convenient locations.

Wearable-based disease management is emerging as a transformative technology. The biosensor-integrated contact lens glucose monitoring market is projected to reach $10.45 billion by 2035, with research and development in non-invasive glucose monitoring accelerating significantly. This technology has the potential to fundamentally reshape how chronic diseases are managed in clinical practice.

Efforts to enhance healthcare access are expanding on multiple fronts domestically and internationally. Japan International Clinic KYOTO, offering 44-language support for international patients, is scheduled to open in Kyoto in November, addressing longstanding barriers to healthcare access for visitors to Japan. These developments demonstrate that technological advancement in healthcare and democratization of medical access are advancing in parallel.
